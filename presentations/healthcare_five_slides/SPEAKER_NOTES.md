## 1. 1 | High ROE is concentrated in a few companies

Opening question: Why do healthcare companies differ in ROE and what does that imply for sustainable performance? ROE = annual net income / year-end book equity ×100. 20% means 20 cents annual profit per dollar of book equity, not stock return. Chart uses each of 43 firms’ average across five or six usable fiscal years. Sample SD of these company averages is 21.90 percentage points. IQR=Q3−Q1; ROE outliers beyond Q1−1.5IQR or Q3+1.5IQR are ABBV, AMGN, IDXX, LLY, ZTS. Excluding them gives mean13.50%, n38; this is sensitivity, not proof of bad data. Distribution is right-skewed; do not assume normality. We omitted the Q–Q graphic to keep the presentation focused.

## 2. 2 | Business-model groups follow different ROE paths

Each bar is the median ROE of available eligible firms in that draft group and fiscal year. All charts share one vertical scale. Labels give minimum–maximum annual sample count. Group mapping is provisional, not an independently validated classification. Company membership can vary by year. Pharma revenue depends on product sales, competition and patent protection; providers sell care/services; insurers receive premiums or service fees while bearing medical/admin costs; tools/device firms sell equipment, consumables or services. These are contextual business-model descriptions, not causal estimates from these bars. Care/diagnostic services mixes UHS with diagnostic laboratories: do not call this a hospitals-only comparison. The CSV provides every bar and count; avoid significance claims for small selected groups.

## 4. 4 | High ROE can reflect a small equity denominator

This is a close-up chosen after the full-sample analysis, not a claim that only two firms were studied. Amgen: 20.98% margin ×0.4057 turnover ×10.4627 multiplier=89.06% ROE. IDEXX:24.62%×1.2844×2.0872=65.99%. Net income/assets is8.51% versus31.62%. Equity multiplier=assets/equity, not debt/equity. Higher multiplier magnifies positive asset return; it does not by itself prove superior operations. To determine why equity is small, inspect equity rollforwards, buybacks, losses and distributions. These are accounting identities, not independent causal predictors.
